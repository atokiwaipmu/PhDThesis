We investigate the influence of the super-sample effect on the mean and covariance of the power spectrum and higher-order statistics using two distinct simulation approaches: BIGBOX and TILED. Our analysis draws on 11 realizations from the state-of-the-art HalfDome project, each containing $6144^3$ particles within a $3750~\mathrm{Mpc}/h$ volume, as well as 20 TILED realizations, each originating from smaller $1024^3$-particle boxes spanning $625~\mathrm{Mpc}/h$. Convergence fields are constructed under the Born approximation. To achieve uniform sky coverage, we employ a Fibonacci grid scheme to extract $10^{\circ} \times 10^{\circ}$ patches and subsequently evaluate a comprehensive set of higher-order statistics, including the bispectrum, probability distribution function (PDF), peak and minima counts, and Minkowski functionals, together with the angular power spectrum. These metrics enable a robust assessment of non-Gaussian features in convergence maps and reveal the impact of the super-sample effect.

\at{Our comparative analysis shows that mean summary statistics from BIGBOX and TILED simulations agree to within $1\%$ across all statistics, redshifts, and multipoles, thereby confirming both simulations' fidelity to the underlying cosmological model. In contrast, variance ratios exhibit notable discrepancies of $10\%-30\%$, particularly at higher multipoles and redshifts, driven by large-scale modes present in BIGBOX but missing in TILED. Although shape noise affects several observables, the super-sample effect remains the dominant factor. Gaussian smoothing modifies small-scale structures but does not significantly alter covariance or correlation properties. Box replication artifacts, however, introduce additional systematic biases and increase variance, underscoring the need for careful simulation design and execution.}

\at{These findings underscore the importance of accurately modeling the super-sample effect for high-precision cosmological analyses, especially in light of upcoming weak-lensing surveys such as LSST, Euclid, and Roman. A reliable variance estimate mandates the inclusion of large-scale modes while ensuring robustness against shape noise and smoothing procedures. Mitigating systematic challenges like box replication artifacts is essential to avoid biased interpretations of higher-order statistics. Future efforts will focus on resolving these artifacts, incorporating diverse cosmological models and baryonic physics, and integrating refined approaches into survey design and data analysis pipelines to bolster cosmological parameter inference. Our study marks a critical step toward a deeper understanding of the super-sample effect and its implications for generating and analyzing next-generation weak-lensing mock data.}
